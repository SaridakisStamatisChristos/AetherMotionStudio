## ART-01 choreography authoring pattern (2026-11-25)
Fixture pair `examples/art01-product-{cpu,three}-polished.json` re-choreographs the AF01 `product` shot (same smartphone, copy, environment, fidelity, geometry profile, 180 frames) using only existing tracks, presets and action modes — no engine change. Tests: tests/art01-choreography.test.js.
- **Primary arc in tracks.** Anticipation and overshoot are extra keyframes (counter-turn before the main turn; overshoot then settle). Each keyframe segment is smoothstep-eased, so velocity is zero at every keyframe — natural at extremes (anticipation/overshoot), but a multi-key path that should flow through a keyframe will visibly pause there.
- **Layered actions on free channels.** `composePose` skips an action channel the asset's `tracks` already define. A `once` action stops contributing when its window closes, so the channel snaps back to the base pose: only end an action window on the channel's base value (e.g. `settle-in`, `reveal-spin` scale, `emphasis`), or use `blend` to fade it. The test suite pins this snap (negative test) so a future engine change is noticed.
- **Secondary motion.** `hover` in `loop` mode with `weight` < 1 and `blend` gives a soft bob that fades in and lands back at contact.
- Result: preflight meanProjectedCenterMotion 0.83 → 19.8 (LOW_PROJECTED_MOTION cleared, no new issues); the AF01 fixture and its finding are unchanged. Known limitation: subject stays small in frame (AF01 camera distance; zoom near limit) — composition belongs to ART-02.

## ART-01 slice 2 — opt-in interpolation and held actions (contract additions, 2026-11-25)
All three are optional; absent fields keep the historical behaviour exactly (graph-state digest over all 48 examples, 980 evaluations of 140 graph shots, identical before/after: a8db8a25…). Both backends consume the same evaluated graph state, so parity is unchanged.
- `assets[].interpolation`: `{channel: 'ease'|'flow'}` for channels that have a track (after motion-preset resolution). `ease` = per-segment smoothstep (default; stops at every key). `flow` = monotone cubic Hermite (Fritsch–Butland/Brodlie tangents): passes through keys, keeps velocity through monotone interior keys, flat at ends and local extrema, never overshoots neighbouring keys (so limits still hold). With two keys, or when every interior key is an extremum, `flow` equals `ease`.
- `shots[].cameraInterpolation` (cinematic-graph only): same map for camera `x`,`y`,`z`,`zoom` tracks; honoured with and without `cameraRig`.
- `actions[].hold: true` (not with `loop`): after the window ends the action keeps its end pose instead of snapping back to the base; `blend` then fades in only. Order still matters: later actions blend over held earlier ones.
- Validation is explicit (unknown mode, channel without a track, empty/array map, `hold` not `true`, `hold` with loop, field on non-graph shots → rejected with the field path). Tests: tests/art01-engine.test.js. Demonstration pair: `examples/art01-flow-{cpu,three}-polished.json` (flowing x drift and camera push-in, held final lift).

## ART-01 slice 3 — interaction: handoff chains, release, facing (contract additions, 2026-11-25)
All optional; absent fields keep historical behaviour (graph-state and preflight-report digests over all 48 pre-existing examples identical before/after: graph 7a2492bc…, preflight 8d2645cf…, 966 evaluations).
- `attachment` may be an array of 2–4 strictly sequential windows (handoff chain). Window 0 uses the historical formula; from window 1 the prop blends from where it was released (`blendIn`) into the new hand and keeps its world yaw as handed over, then turns with the new holder. Between windows it stays frozen at detach. Cycles across any window are rejected.
- `release` on the last window (or a single window): `{mode:'drop',duration}` falls straight down with ease-in (gravity-like) to the floor (y 0); `{mode:'place',to:[x,y,z],duration}` eases onto an explicit point (y 0–1.5, e.g. a table top). Must complete within the shot. Absent = historical freeze in mid-air.
- `assets[].face`: `{target,start,end,blend?,hold?,offset?}` turns yaw toward another asset's own track/action position (not its attachment-resolved position), shortest way, blended; `offset` (−π..π) adds a body turn (e.g. to bring right hands together). Mutual facing is allowed.
- Preflight `HANDOFF_GAP` (warning, limit 0.3 world units) checks the giver/receiver hand distance at each chain handoff; single attachments are not checked, so historical reports are unchanged.
- Identity continuity ledger does not yet model chains: an identity-tracked prop or holder in a chain is rejected explicitly ("handoff chains are not yet supported by the identity continuity ledger").
- Fixture `examples/art01-handoff-{cpu,three}-polished.json`: engineer hands a wrench to a service robot, which turns and places it on a table; a retro-robot watches. Positions/pitches/offsets were solved numerically for a 0.05 hand gap; table placement solved for clearance and a 1.7 cm set-down. Tests: tests/art01-interaction.test.js.

## POLISH-01 — planted walking, interaction solver, staging art-v2 (2026-11-25, opt-in)
- `assets[].locomotion: {mode:'stride', start, end, faceTravel?, blend?}` (src/core/locomotion.js) for legged catalog humans/robots. The leg cycle is driven by distance travelled along the asset's own root path (fixed 240-step grid), not time: triangle-wave stride of length 4 × leg swing (human 0.2, robot 0.17) × scale × depth, so the stance foot moves backward exactly as fast as the body moves forward; the swing foot lifts (`footLift`, geometry only when present). `faceTravel` turns the body toward its direction of travel (blended at the window edges). After the window a closing step (SETTLE = 0.06 progress) eases the legs to standing. Rejected with walking tracks or walk/run actions. Measured stance-foot slip: ~0 on straight travel (legacy `walk` action slides ~2 cm/frame); 1–2 cm during body turns (pivot on the planted foot — expected).
- `src/core/interaction.js solveHandoff(giver, receiver, {giverAt, scale, side})`: deterministic analytic grid search over receiver offset, face offsets and arm pitches; hands meet within ~1–6 cm for human/robot pairs while both characters stay ≤100° from the +z camera. Used by staging art-v2 and the extended film.
- Staging `art-v2` (src/core/staging.js) = art-v1 plus: an in-place `walk`/`run` on a legged catalog rig becomes a planted walk across its spot; two free characters + a free hand-carryable prop (≤1.3 tall, ≤0.9 wide) become a solved handoff at 0.55 (prop scaled to ≤0.7). art-v1 output is unchanged.
